Part 7A

Grain & Agriculture

From country elevator to export terminal — the biggest rail commodity

Grain Rail Supply Chain

  • Country elevator → shuttle train → export terminal or domestic processor
  • Shuttle-capable facilities: 100+ car loading/unloading in under 15 hours
  • Covered hoppers are the standard car: 263,000 or 286,000 lb gross weight
  • Shuttle trains get the lowest rates — 10-15% discount vs. single-car
  • USDA grading and fumigation requirements must be met before loading
  • Seasonal capacity crunch: harvest (Sep-Nov) and planting (Mar-May) are peak
  • Grain moves are often contract-based with annual volume commitments
  • Key routes: Midwest → Gulf Coast (export), Midwest → Southeast (poultry feed)

Grain by the Numbers

Annual Rail Volume. 1.6B tons. Grain is #2 rail commodity by tonnage. 90%+ moves in covered hoppers. Export drives 60% of volume. Ethanol is the growth segment. Shuttle Train Savings. 10-15%. 100+ car trains cycle continuously. Elevator must load in <15 hours. Railroads prioritize shuttle lanes. Highest efficiency = lowest rate. Seasonal Swing. 30-40%. Harvest = max demand on car supply. Car shortages are common Sep-Nov. Pre-position cars before harvest. Storage costs spike if rail is slow.

Part 7B

Chemicals & Hazmat

High-value, high-regulation — where compliance is non-negotiable

Chemical Rail Transport Essentials

  • Tank cars are the primary equipment: DOT 111, DOT 117 (enhanced safety), pressurized
  • DOT 117 required for Class 3 flammable liquids (crude oil, ethanol) since 2025
  • Pressurized tank cars for LPG, chlorine, anhydrous ammonia — specialized and expensive. 49 CFR Parts 171-180 govern HAZMAT classification, packaging, marking, and placarding
  • Every shipment needs: shipping papers, placards, emergency response info, and proper marks
  • FRA HM-263: real-time train consist reporting required by 2026 — shippers must provide accurate data
  • Responsible Care program: chemical industry's voluntary safety management system
  • Insurance and liability costs are significantly higher for hazmat rail movements

Tank Car Types: Know Your Equipment

  • Non-Pressurized (DOT 111/117)
  • Crude oil, ethanol, vegetable oil, corn syrup
  • DOT 117: thicker shell, thermal protection, top-fitting protection. 20,000-30,000 gallon capacity typical
  • Must be inspected every 10 years (HM-201)
  • Fleet transition to DOT 117 largely complete
  • Lease rates: $600-$900/month
  • Pressurized (DOT 105/112/114)
  • LPG, chlorine, anhydrous ammonia, butadiene
  • Higher design pressure (100-500 psi)
  • Specialized fittings, safety valves, relief devices
  • More expensive to build and maintain
  • Shorter supply — plan ahead for fleet needs
  • Lease rates: $900-$1,500/month
Part 7C

Aggregates & Construction

Sand, gravel, crushed stone — high volume, low margin, transload-dependent

Aggregates Rail Logistics

  • Primarily moves in open-top hoppers and gondolas — gravity unloading
  • Very high tonnage, very low value per ton — rail economics must work hard
  • Transloading is essential: quarry → rail → transload facility → truck to job site
  • Construction season drives demand (April-October in most markets)
  • Weight limits are critical: 263,000 lb or 286,000 lb gross — overweight = penalties + track damage
  • Rapid unloading is key: bottom-dump hoppers at 100+ tons per car
  • Frac sand for oil/gas drilling became huge (2015-2020), now stabilized
  • Aggregates are a short-haul sweet spot — rail competitive at 100-500 miles

Aggregate Car Types & Economics

  • Open-Top Hoppers
  • Most common for sand/gravel/stone
  • Bottom dump for fast unloading. 100-115 ton capacity per car
  • Gravity unload = no special equipment
  • Lease: $400-$600/month
  • Best for: bulk dry aggregates. 🚢
  • Flat-bottom, open-top, high sides
  • More versatile than hoppers
  • Can carry odd-sized material (rip-rap, boulders)
  • Must use crane/clam for unloading. 90-100 ton capacity typical
  • Best for: mixed aggregate, scrap, rip-rap. ⚡
  • Rapid Discharge Cars
  • Specialized bottom dump design
  • Can unload 100 cars in 2-3 hours
  • Used in dedicated unit train service
  • Higher lease cost but faster turns
  • Fewer demurrage issues
  • Best for: high-volume, repeat routes
Part 7D

Coal, Ethanol & Energy

Legacy coal, growing renewables, and drilling minerals — energy moves by rail

Coal: What You Need to Know If You Ship It

  • Unit train operations: 100-135 cars, mine to power plant, dedicated consist
  • Coal is declining (-40% from 2008 peak) but still 15-20% of Class I revenue
  • Car type: aluminum or steel open-top hoppers, rotary dump compatible
  • PRB coal moves 1,000+ miles — long haul is where rail dominates
  • Power plant unloading: rotary dumpers process 100+ cars in 4-6 hours
  • Existing coal shippers: focus on contract renewals — railroads want to keep the volume they have
  • New shippers entering coal: unlikely — natural gas displacement is permanent
  • Export coal via Norfolk/Baltimore is the one growth area — driven by overseas demand

Ethanol & Renewable Fuels: The Growth Commodity

  • Ethanol is the fastest-growing rail energy commodity — federal RFS mandates drive demand
  • DOT 117 tank cars REQUIRED for ethanol (Class 3 flammable liquid) — no exceptions
  • Shuttle train operations: 80-100 tank cars cycling between ethanol plants and blending terminals
  • Blending terminals receive ethanol by rail, mix with gasoline, and distribute by truck
  • Key routes: Midwest corn belt → Gulf Coast refineries, East Coast blending terminals
  • Renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) are emerging rail commodities
  • Lease rates for DOT 117 tank cars: $600-$900/month — plan fleet needs 6-12 months ahead
  • HAZMAT compliance: 49 CFR applies, placarding required, emergency response plans mandatory

Frac Sand & Drilling Minerals: The Transload Commodity

  • Frac sand (silica proppant) is essential for hydraulic fracturing in oil & gas drilling
  • Wisconsin, Illinois, and Texas are the primary sand sources — moves 500-1,500 miles by rail
  • Open-top hoppers and covered hoppers are the standard equipment (100-ton loads)
  • Highly transload-dependent: rail to transload yard near wellhead, then truck to drill site
  • Demand is cyclical: tied to oil prices, rig counts, and completion activity
  • Peak frac sand demand hit 100M+ tons in 2018-2019 — stabilized but still significant volume
  • Unit train and multi-car block service available on major lanes (Wisconsin→Permian Basin)
  • Key risk for shippers: rapid demand swings can strand leased car fleets during downturns
Part 7E

Forest Products & Lumber

From sawmill to distribution — boxcars and centerbeams

Forest Products Rail Shipping

Lumber & Building Materials. Centerbeam flatcars are the primary equipment. Lumber, plywood, OSB, dimensional timber. Load securement per AAR standards is critical. Seasonal demand tied to construction (spring/summer). Distribution center delivery via rail + truck transload. Competitive with truck at 500+ miles. Pulp, Paper & Packaging. Boxcars protect moisture-sensitive products. Newsprint, linerboard, corrugated, tissue. Paper mills often have direct rail service. Large roll sizes require special loading techniques. Palletized freight loaded with forklifts. Covered gondolas for wood chips to mills.

Part 7F

Steel & Metals

Heavy, valuable, and demanding — precision loading for maximum payload

Steel & Metals Rail Logistics

  • Coil cars: specialized cradle design for steel coils — prevents rolling and damage
  • Gondolas: flat-bottom for steel beams, plates, structural shapes, scrap
  • Flatcars: oversized loads, heavy equipment, pipe, structural steel
  • Weight distribution is critical — improper loading causes derailments and penalties
  • Securement standards (AAR Circular OT-55) are strictly enforced — liability is on the shipper
  • Mill-to-customer: steel mills ship direct to OEMs, fabricators, service centers
  • Scrap-to-mill: scrap yards ship to mini-mills and integrated mills (gondola loads)
  • Steel is high-value freight — railroads compete hard for these lanes

Steel Car Types & Loading

  • Purpose-built cradles for steel coils
  • Eye-to-sky or eye-to-side loading
  • Coil weights: 10,000-50,000 lbs each
  • Blocking and bracing per AAR rules
  • Covers available for weather protection
  • Lease: $500-$800/month. 🏗️
  • Flat-bottom, open-top versatility
  • Steel beams, plates, rebar, scrap
  • Crane loading/unloading required
  • Weight distribution across car floor
  • Dunnage to prevent shifting
  • Lease: $400-$600/month. 🛤️
  • For oversized steel products and pipe
  • Structural steel, H-beams, large plate
  • Requires chains, straps, and blocking
  • Oversize/overweight permits may be needed
  • Special handling increases transit time
  • Lease: $350-$550/month

Key Terms — Commodity Logistics

  • Shuttle Train
  • Dedicated consist cycling between origin-destination, 100+ cars, fastest loading
  • Enhanced safety tank car for flammable liquids — thicker shell, thermal protection
  • Rotary Dumper
  • Power plant equipment that flips railcars to unload coal — 100+ cars in hours
  • Centerbeam Flatcar
  • Specialized car for lumber/building materials with center partition
  • Railcar with cradle designed specifically for transporting steel coils safely. 286K Car
  • Railcar rated at 286,000 lbs gross weight — the modern standard capacity
  • Frac Sand (Proppant)
  • Silica sand used in hydraulic fracturing — moves by rail to transload near drill sites
  • Constructive Placement
  • Car considered placed even if held at nearby yard due to full siding

Key Takeaways

✅ Every commodity has specific car types — using the wrong one costs money or creates safety issues. ✅ Grain, coal, and ethanol are unit-train commodities — volume unlocks massive rate discounts. ✅ Chemicals require strict DOT/FRA compliance — cutting corners is not an option. ✅ Aggregates are transload-dependent — the last mile determines if rail pencils out. ✅ Steel is high-value and weight-sensitive — proper loading prevents derailments and claims. ✅ Seasonal patterns drive capacity: plan your car supply 60-90 days ahead of peak season. ✅ Forest products are competitive with truck at 500+ miles — rail wins on long haul.
