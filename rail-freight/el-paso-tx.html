Rail Logistics Services in El Paso, TX

2 Class I Railroads · 3 Major Terminals

El Paso is one of the busiest U.S.-Mexico rail border crossings, with BNSF and Union Pacific operating international gateways that connect to Mexican rail networks. Cross-border trade drives massive merchandise and automotive volumes. The city is a critical link in the supply chain connecting Mexican manufacturing with U.S. markets.

Rail logistics services in El Paso

A El Paso shipper moving automotive, consumer goods and copper by rail can route on BNSF or Union Pacific, and the two carriers rarely price the same lane the same way. Steel Wheel Logistics is that shipper's outsourced rail department: we price the lane, choose the routing between BNSF and UP and handle the railroad coordination that most plants no longer staff in-house. For automotive, consumer goods, copper and building materials that means sourcing autoracks and boxcars, managing car ordering and supply, and watching demurrage before it accrues. Plants without their own rail spur can still ship by rail through a transload site near El Paso (see the facilities below), riding rail for the long haul and truck for the last few miles. We are a national company based in Petal, Mississippi, and work El Paso lanes remotely, by phone and email, the same way shippers work with the railroads themselves. Railroads Serving El Paso

El Paso is served by BNSF and Union Pacific, providing solid Class I railroad coverage for freight shippers in the El Paso metro area.

BNSF

BNSF Railway operates one of the largest rail networks in North America, covering the western two-thirds of the United States. BNSF is a major carrier for coal, grain, and industrial products.

Union Pacific

Union Pacific Railroad operates the largest rail network in the U.S., spanning 23 states across the western two-thirds of the country. UP handles diverse freight including industrial, coal, and agricultural products.

El Paso, TX is served by BNSF and Union Pacific, with major facilities including El Paso Yard and Alfalfa Yard. Short lines and switching carriers connect individual plants and transload sites to those Class I networks.
