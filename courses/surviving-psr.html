Part A

Understanding PSR

What it is, why it happened, and what it means for shippers

What Is Precision Scheduled Railroading?

  • Operating philosophy created by Hunter Harrison (Illinois Central, CN, CSX, CP)
  • Core principle: move cars on a fixed schedule, not when a train is 'full enough'
  • Every asset (car, locomotive, crew, track) must be utilized to maximum efficiency
  • Reduces classification yard operations — cars go point-to-point where possible
  • Fewer, longer trains replace many shorter trains
  • Aggressive cost reduction: headcount, yards, assets, complexity
  • Wall Street LOVES it: operating ratios dropped from 70%+ to sub-60%
  • Adopted by ALL 6 Class I railroads between 2012-2020

PSR Impact: What It Costs YOU (2017-2025)

  • Transit Time. +20-40%
  • Carload shipments take longer
  • Fewer trains, longer routes
  • Yard closures add miles
  • Plan for 7-14 day manifest moves
  • Yards Closed. ~100+
  • Classification yards shuttered
  • Fewer sorting points
  • Longer yard-to-yard hauls
  • Some reopened under pressure
  • Demurrage Costs. +71%
  • Industry-wide surge in charges
  • Aggressive constructive placement
  • Less free time at many facilities
  • Avg shipper: $50K-$200K/yr more
  • Carload Volume. -15%
  • Manifest traffic declined
  • Some shippers left for truck
  • Service quality dropped
  • PSR shed 'unprofitable' traffic

How PSR Prioritizes Your Traffic

Traffic Type. PSR Priority. Avg Transit. Rate Trend. Multimodal. HIGHEST. Scheduled. Stable. Unit Train. HIGH. 2-5 days. Competitive. Multi-Car Block. MEDIUM. 5-8 days. Rising. Single Carload. LOW. 7-14 days. Rising fast. Short-Haul Carload. LOWEST. 7-21 days. Pushed to truck.

"Rates Are Up and Service Is Down" — The Shipper Experience

  • TRANSIT TIME: Carload shipments take 20-40% longer than pre-PSR
  • RELIABILITY: On-time performance has dropped; ETAs are less trustworthy
  • DEMURRAGE: Charges up 71% as railroads monetize dwell more aggressively
  • CAR SUPPLY: Fewer railroad-owned cars available; private car fleets now essential
  • SWITCHING: Some local service reduced to 3x/week or less (was daily)
  • YARD CLOSURES: Your car may now travel 200+ extra miles to reach a yard
  • SMALL SHIPPERS: Hit hardest — not enough volume to get attention
  • The fundamental tension: railroads optimized for shareholders, not shippers
Part B

Adapting Your Operations

Practical strategies for surviving and thriving under PSR

PSR Impact & Shipper Response

  • What PSR Costs Shippers
  • Demurrage up 71% — longer cycles, tighter free time
  • Transit adds 20-40% — manifest moves now 7-14 days
  • Inventory carrying costs up — need more safety stock
  • Car supply squeezed — must lease private fleet
  • Truck backup spend — critical lanes need Plan B
  • Lost customers — service failures drive buyers away. ⚙️
  • Operational Wins You Control
  • Faster car turns cut demurrage immediately
  • Predictable loading windows earn railroad priority
  • Buffer inventory absorbs transit variability
  • Private fleet leasing gives car supply independence
  • Pre-stage inbound/outbound to reduce dwell to zero
  • Invest in siding upgrades for faster throughput. 💪
  • How to Win Back Rate & Service
  • Volume commitments unlock 10-20% rate discounts
  • Service-level agreements with penalty clauses
  • Short-line alternatives for carload freight
  • STB complaint leverage (R/VC 180% threshold)
  • Document every failure — data wins negotiations
  • Join NITL/MARS for collective shipper voice

Working With Your Railroad Rep Under PSR

  • What They CAN Do
  • Escalate specific service issues
  • Negotiate rate structures and terms
  • Provide car supply commitments (with volume)
  • Adjust placement schedules
  • Waive or reduce demurrage (case by case)
  • Connect you with operations team for problem-solving
  • What They CAN'T Do
  • Override the operating plan (trains run on schedule)
  • Guarantee manifest transit times (too many variables)
  • Provide unlimited car supply (fleet is sized tighter)
  • Reopen closed yards or restore daily switching
  • Lower rates below their cost floor
  • Change PSR — it's a corporate philosophy, not a policy

The Short-Line Alternative

  • Short-line railroads often provide BETTER carload service than Class I under PSR
  • They're in the carload business — it's their entire revenue model
  • More flexible switching schedules (daily service is common)
  • Better communication and responsiveness (you know the GM by name)
  • Can provide transload and terminal services that Class I's have cut
  • Often competitive on rates for short-haul and regional moves. 603 short lines across North America — check if one serves your area
  • Limitation: they interchange with Class I for long-haul, so transit time still depends on the Class I

The STB's Role: Your Regulatory Backstop

  • Surface Transportation Board: federal agency regulating railroad rates and service
  • Common Carrier Obligation: railroads MUST provide service to any shipper who requests it
  • Rate Reasonableness Challenge: captive shippers can challenge rates above R/VC 180%
  • Competitive Switching: proposed rule allowing shipper access to a second carrier ($3-5K switching fee)
  • Reciprocal Switching: Canada already has this; US is debating it under current STB chair
  • Service complaints: STB can investigate and order railroads to improve service
  • Reality check: STB proceedings are expensive ($500K-$2M+) and slow (years)
  • But the THREAT of an STB complaint has negotiating value — railroads know the rules

Shipper Advocacy Organizations

🏛️. NITL. National Industrial Transportation League. Largest shipper advocacy group. Lobbies Congress and STB. Annual conference with railroads. Rate and service benchmarking. Legal resources for rate challenges. 🏭. MARS. Manufacturers Alliance for Rail Shippers. Focus on captive shipper issues. Competitive switching advocacy. Coalition with agricultural shippers. Testifies at STB hearings. Strong chemical industry participation. 🗺️. NEARS. NE Association of Rail Shippers. Regional focus (NE corridor). Ports and multimodal issues. Smaller, more accessible. Good networking for eastern shippers. Annual meeting and workshops.

PSR Survival Checkpoint

✓ You understand what PSR is and why railroads adopted it. ✓ You know how PSR prioritizes traffic (multimodal > unit > carload). ✓ You can adapt your operations: faster turns, buffer inventory, predictable loading. ✓ You know when short-line railroads are a better option. ✓ You understand the STB's role and when to use it as leverage. ✓ You know the major shipper advocacy organizations and their focus.

Key Terms

  • Precision Scheduled Railroading — efficiency-focused operating model
  • Operating Ratio
  • Operating expenses / revenue; PSR targets sub-60%
  • Manifest Train
  • Mixed-commodity train; most affected by PSR cuts
  • Competitive Switching
  • Proposed rule allowing access to a second railroad carrier
  • Common Carrier Obligation
  • Railroad's legal duty to serve any shipper requesting service
  • Surface Transportation Board — federal railroad regulator
  • National Industrial Transportation League — shipper advocacy
  • Hunter Harrison
  • Railroad executive who created PSR (IC, CN, CSX, CP)
  • Reciprocal Switching
  • Mandatory interchange access between carriers (exists in Canada)

Practical Assignment

  • Step 1: Collect your rail service data: transit times, on-time %, demurrage, car supply issues
  • Step 2: Calculate the financial impact of PSR on your operations (vs. 3 years ago)
  • Step 3: Identify which of your lanes are most affected by PSR (longest delays, highest demurrage)
  • Step 4: Research short-line alternatives for your worst-performing lanes
  • Step 5: Draft a service improvement request letter to your railroad representative
  • Step 6: Join (or research) NITL or MARS — attend their next meeting or webinar
  • Step 7: Build a 90-day PSR adaptation plan with measurable targets
  • Step 8: Present your findings and plan to your supply chain leadership team

Key Takeaways

✅ PSR is permanent — every Class I has adopted it and Wall Street demands it continue. ✅ Carload shippers are the most affected: longer transit, higher demurrage, less reliability. ✅ Adapt your operations: faster car turns, buffer inventory, predictable loading windows. ✅ Short-line railroads are often the best option for carload freight under PSR. ✅ Document every service failure — you need data for negotiations and STB complaints. ✅ The STB is your regulatory backstop, but proceedings are expensive and slow. ✅ Shipper advocacy matters — NITL, MARS, and NEARS give you a collective voice. ✅ The best defense is operational excellence + commercial leverage + regulatory awareness.
