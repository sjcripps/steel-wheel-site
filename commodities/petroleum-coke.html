Petroleum Coke Rail Shipping & Logistics: Rates, Railcars, Lanes

The bottom-of-the-barrel refinery byproduct — fuel-grade petcoke moves like coal, anode-grade feeds aluminum smelters.

Petroleum coke logistics by rail: what we do

Steel Wheel Logistics prices and coordinates petroleum coke carload moves nationwide — single cars and multi-car blocks — from routing and an indicative rate per car through car ordering, release and tracing to placement. Petroleum coke weighs out at 100–115 tons per car (roughly 45–55 lb per cubic foot) well before the car is full, so we size open-top hopper or rotary gondolas to the weight limit and the receiving track's capacity rather than to cube. Typical end uses are cement kilns and power generation (fuel grade), aluminum anodes (calcined grade), export — we confirm the receiving track and unloading method before the first car is ordered. Where the plant has no spur, we set up transload at a bulk yard near the receiver and price the rail-plus-truck move against straight truck, so the mode decision is made on numbers. Demurrage control is planned rather than reactive: manifest cars arrive bunched, so we set release rules with the receiver and watch free time car by car. Shippers who need equipment can use our railcar brokerage to source open-top hopper or rotary gondolas on lease or per trip.

Common questions

Can petroleum coke be shipped by rail?

Yes. Petroleum Coke moves regularly on the North American rail network in open-top hopper or rotary gondolas. Gulf Coast refineries to cement plants, export docks and calciners; handled with coal-style rotary dump equipment..

How many tons of petroleum coke fit in a railcar?

A typical load is 100–115 net tons per car — the car reaches its weight limit well before its cubic capacity.

What railcar does petroleum coke move in?

Open-top hopper or rotary gondola. Because the commodity is dense, smaller-cube cars are common — the weight limit governs.

What is the bulk density of petroleum coke?

Roughly 45–55 lb per cubic foot, depending on grade and condition.
