Free Astray Rail Cars: How the Railroad-Replaces-at-No-Charge Rule Works (2026)

May 16, 2026 · 7 min read · Operations
Key fact: When a railroad misroutes a car — sends it to the wrong destination, strands it on a wrong line, or delivers it to the wrong consignee due to railroad error — the AAR-adopted "free astray" rule entitles the shipper to have the car corrected and delivered to the right destination at no additional charge. Shippers underclaim this regularly because they don't know the rule exists.

"Astray" is the rail-industry term for a car that ended up somewhere it wasn't supposed to be due to carrier error. The AAR / railroad operating rules establish that the railroad fixes its own mistakes on its own dime — you don't pay for the railroad's misrouting. Here's the rule, how to invoke it, and the BoL paper trail that supports the claim.

What Counts as "Astray"

A car is astray when its actual location or routing diverges from the routing instructions on the BoL because of carrier action (or inaction), not shipper error. Common scenarios:

  • Switched onto the wrong outbound train at a classification yard
  • Interchange handoff to the wrong delivering carrier
  • Delivered to the wrong customer track at destination
  • Routed through an unauthorized junction (where Rule 11 didn't apply)
  • Lost in the yard system — sitting in a hold track for weeks with no apparent reason

NOT astray:

  • Shipper put the wrong destination on the BoL
  • Shipper made a routing error (wrong junction code)
  • Diversion/reconsignment was requested but the car hasn't moved yet
  • Car is delayed but on the correct route

The "Free Astray" Rule

The AAR / railroad customer service rules (as published in carrier tariffs and reflected in industry practice) provide that when a car is astray due to railroad error, the railroad will:

  1. Identify the error and the correct routing
  2. Return the car to its proper route at no charge to the shipper or consignee
  3. Absorb any backhaul mileage, handling, switching, or per-diem charges associated with the correction
  4. Adjust any accessorial charges that resulted from the carrier error (e.g., demurrage charged due to misplacement)

"Free" specifically means no additional freight charges; the shipper still owes the original BoL freight charges (rated to the original destination per the original BoL).

How to Identify a Free Astray Situation

Two diagnostic patterns:

Tracking signal mismatch

The carrier's car-tracing portal shows the car at a location that doesn't match the BoL routing. For example: BoL says origin Chicago to destination Atlanta via Memphis junction (NS); car traces to Birmingham (CSX territory) with no diversion request on file. Smoking-gun pattern: the trace events show a switching error at the originating yard.

Excessive transit time

A move that should take 7-10 days takes 25+ days with the car bouncing between yards. The trace events show repeated holds, classifications, and re-routings. Often a sign the car was misclassified and is being shuffled until someone figures out where it's supposed to go.

How to Claim Free Astray

Submit to the originating carrier's customer service desk:

  1. BoL number, car initial + number, original BoL date
  2. Statement of the routing instructions (BoL destination, junctions, delivering carrier)
  3. Tracking history showing the divergence (screenshot or print from carrier portal)
  4. Request for "free astray correction" with the specific correct routing
  5. Request for waiver of any demurrage or accessorial charges incurred due to the error

The carrier acknowledges (within 1-3 business days), works the correction, and confirms when the car is back on the right route. Demurrage and accessorial reversals usually require a separate claim filing, often within 30-60 days of the incident.

BoL and Documentation

The original BoL stands as the documentary record of the intended routing. No BoL amendment is needed because the shipper didn't change the destination — the railroad simply has to execute what the BoL already says. Preserve:

  • The original BoL with full routing
  • The tracking history showing the divergence
  • Communications with the carrier customer service desk
  • Any demurrage notices issued during the misplacement

What If the Railroad Refuses?

Carrier customer service may push back on free astray claims, arguing the situation isn't astray or that some part of the error was shipper-caused. Escalation paths:

  • Account manager / sales rep at the originating carrier
  • Customer service supervisor
  • STB informal complaint (the Surface Transportation Board accepts shipper complaints about carrier service)
  • Carmack claim for damages if the misrouting caused commercial loss (spoilage, cancelled contracts, demurrage that wasn't waived)

Best Practice

Build an internal protocol for monitoring car movement against BoL routing. Set automated alerts when transit time exceeds expected lane time by >50%. Most shippers don't catch free astray situations because they don't watch the trace data closely enough.

For shippers running 50+ cars/month, an in-house dispatcher or contracted 3PL with a car-tracing dashboard is cheap insurance. The first free-astray correction caught typically pays for the dashboard for a year.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a free astray rail car?

A car that ended up at the wrong destination, on the wrong line, or with the wrong consignee due to carrier error (not shipper error). Under AAR / railroad customer service rules, the railroad returns the car to its proper route at no additional freight charge to the shipper and absorbs the backhaul mileage, switching, and per-diem associated with the correction.

Who pays the freight when a car is astray?

The shipper still owes the original BoL freight charges (rated to the original destination per the original routing). The railroad absorbs any additional movement, handling, and accessorials caused by its own error. The "free" in free astray means no additional charge, not zero charge.

How do I claim free astray on a misrouted car?

Submit to the originating carrier's customer service desk: BoL number, car initial + number, BoL date, the original routing instructions, tracking history showing the divergence, and a request for free astray correction. Request waiver of any demurrage or accessorial charges incurred due to the misrouting. The carrier acknowledges in 1-3 business days and works the correction.

When is a misrouted car NOT eligible for free astray?

When the misrouting was caused by shipper error — wrong destination on the BoL, wrong junction code, routing instructions that contradict published interchange agreements, or shipper-requested diversion that was just executed slowly. Free astray covers carrier-caused errors only, not shipper-caused or third-party-caused errors.

How do I spot a free astray situation?

Two diagnostic patterns: (1) the car-tracing portal shows the car at a location that doesn't match the BoL routing (e.g., wrong carrier territory, wrong interchange); (2) transit time exceeds expected lane time by 2-3x with repeated yard holds and reclassifications in the trace events. Build internal alerts for both patterns; most shippers under-claim free astray because they don't monitor trace data closely.

Does free astray include demurrage waiver?

Typically yes — demurrage charged during a misplacement caused by carrier error should be waived as part of the free astray correction. The waiver isn't always automatic; you usually have to request it explicitly when filing the free astray claim and may need to follow up with the carrier's claims department within 30-60 days.
