Rail Pulse — Week Ending April 25, 2026

May 5, 2026 · 3 min read · Rail Pulse

Rail Pulse is Steel Wheel Logistics' weekly read on what actually moved on the U.S. rail network last week. Every figure is sourced from public AAR, STB, EIA, and USDA filings and linked at the bottom. Every word of analysis is anchored to those numbers — no estimates, no extrapolation.

The Number

511,616 total rail units moved in the week ending April 25, 2026, up 1.9% year over year. Intermodal carried the load, posting 281,788 units at plus 4.9% YoY while carloads slipped to 229,828, down 1.5%. Total traffic rose 0.65% week over week from 508,303 units.

Network Health

As of April 29, UP leads on speed at 24.7 mph (green) with dwell of 20.0 hours (green), followed by BNSF at 25.3 mph (green) though its dwell of 22.1 hours tips yellow. CSX runs 23.8 mph (yellow) with dwell of 20.97 hours (green). NS is the outlier at 20.6 mph (red) and dwell of 23.9 hours (yellow), a combination that warrants close transit-time scrutiny on eastern corridors. CPKC sits at 21.6 mph (yellow) and 22.95 hours dwell (yellow); CN dwell of 20.25 hours is green but system speed data was not reported this period.

Color codes: green = speed ≥24 mph or dwell <22 hrs; yellow = speed 21–24 mph or dwell 22–26 hrs; red = speed <21 mph or dwell >26 hrs. Source: STB EP724 weekly Class I performance filings.

Fuel Watch

Highway diesel closed at $5.64 per gallon on May 4, up 5.4% from $5.351 the prior week and extending a climb that began in mid-February. Current mileage-based fuel surcharge factors effective June 1 stand at 61 cents per mile for BNSF, 69 for UP, 76 for NS, 44 for CSXT, 77 for CN, and 74 for CPKC. Next-period factors for BNSF, UP, NS, CSXT, and CN are not yet published; CPKC has confirmed 74 cents per mile through the May 1 to May 15, 2026 period.

Commodity Spotlight

The Other commodities category posted the largest absolute year-over-year swing for the week ending April 25, 2026, falling 9.3% to 8,900 carloads. Coal was the next notable mover, down 7.8% to 55,082 carloads, though its week-over-week trend was modestly positive at plus 0.88%. On the upside, Petroleum and Petroleum Products led gainers at plus 7.7% YoY with 10,479 carloads, even as it shed 6.09% week over week.
