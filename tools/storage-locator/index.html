Storing cars, or getting them out of storage?

Storage is the cost of not deciding. If the cars are yours or on your lease and you do not need them this season, a sublease usually beats paying storage through the term, and most master leases allow it with the lessor's written consent. If you need cars, the stored fleet is where the fastest, cheapest "good enough" equipment sits. Either way, the same desk handles it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does railcar storage cost?

Published market ranges run about $4 to $16 per car per day at off-railroad yards, plus a switch fee of roughly $100 to $300 per car in and out. Class I railroads also bill private empty cars held on their track under their own storage tariffs. Rates vary by yard, car type, hazmat status and term; treat these as planning ranges, not quotes.

Where does this list of railcar storage yards come from?

Every yard is taken from a named public source, mainly operator storage pages, the Commtrex storage marketplace and short-line railroad directories, and carries the source link and the date it was verified. Fields the source did not publish, such as capacity or a phone number, are left blank rather than estimated.

Is it cheaper to sublease idle railcars than to store them?

Usually. A stored car costs daily storage plus switching while it ages toward its interchange limit; a subleased car earns rent. Most master leases allow subleasing with the lessor's written consent. Our Railcar Lease Renewal Desk handles the consent and the placement.
