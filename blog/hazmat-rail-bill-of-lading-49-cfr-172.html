Hazmat Rail Bill of Lading: 49 CFR 172 Compliance Guide (2026)

May 16, 2026 · 12 min read · Hazmat & Compliance
Key fact: Hazmat rail BoLs must carry three federally-required compliance blocks: a verbatim §172.204 shipper certification, a §172.604 live-answered emergency response phone, and a §172.704 employee training certification. PHMSA 2025 penalty max: $99,756 per violation per day, or $238,809 if death or serious injury results.

Shipping hazardous materials by rail is not paperwork-light. Three separate federal requirements stack on top of the standard BoL fields, every one of them carrying six-figure penalty exposure if you skip them. This guide walks through each in detail, including the verbatim regulatory text you can paste straight onto your BoL.

What Triggers the Hazmat Rules

Any commodity classified as hazardous under the Hazardous Materials Regulations (49 CFR Parts 100-185) triggers the hazmat compliance block on a rail BoL. Most commonly: petroleum products (UN1203 gasoline, UN1267 crude oil), chlorine (UN1017), anhydrous ammonia (UN1005), liquefied petroleum gas (UN1075), corrosives (UN1789 hydrochloric acid, UN1830 sulfuric acid), and many resins and chemicals.

The trigger isn't "is it a tank car" — you can move hazmat in boxcars, hoppers, gondolas, and intermodal containers depending on the commodity. The trigger is the HMR classification of what's inside.

Exceptions that suppress the full hazmat compliance block: Limited Quantities, Excepted Quantities, Consumer Commodity ORM-D, and Dry Ice when shipped as a cooling medium. These still get a shipping name on the BoL but don't require the §172.604 emergency phone.

§172.204 Shipper's Certification (Verbatim)

Source: 49 CFR §172.204. The shipper certifies one of two statements. Use Certification 1 for domestic moves, Certification 2 for international or IMDG/IATA-aligned moves.

Certification 1 (Domestic — default for US-only rail moves)

"This is to certify that the above-named materials are properly classified, described, packaged, marked and labeled, and are in proper condition for transportation according to the applicable regulations of the Department of Transportation."

Certification 2 (International)

"I hereby declare that the contents of this consignment are fully and accurately described above by the proper shipping name, and are classified, packaged, marked and labeled/placarded, and are in all respects in proper condition for transport according to applicable international and national governmental regulations."

Signature rules

Per §172.204(c), the certification must be legibly signed by a principal, officer, partner, or employee of the shipper or shipper's agent. Acceptable signature methods:

  • Manual signature (handwritten)
  • Typewriter or mechanical means
  • For electronic transmission, per §172.204(c)(3)(ii): the name of the authorized person in a designated computer field is a valid signature

Paste the chosen certification verbatim onto your BoL. Do not paraphrase. The legal exposure exists precisely because the language is set by regulation. §172.604 Emergency Response Phone (Verbatim)

Source: 49 CFR §172.604. The verbatim requirement:

"A person who offers a hazardous material for transportation must provide a numeric emergency response telephone number, including the area code, for use in an emergency involving the hazardous material. The telephone number must be monitored at all times the hazardous material is in transportation, including storage incidental to transportation."

Live-answer rule

The number must connect to a person who is either (i) knowledgeable of the hazmat being shipped and has comprehensive emergency response and incident mitigation information, or (ii) has immediate access to such a person. The regulation explicitly prohibits:

"A telephone number that requires a call back (such as an answering service, answering machine, or beeper device) does not meet the requirements of this section."

Practical implementation

Most shippers contract with one of four commercial providers:

  • CHEMTREC — 1-800-424-9300, industry-standard, ~$1,200-2,500/yr for small shipper tier
  • 3E (Verisk 3E) — ~$1,500-3,500/yr
  • INFOTRAC — ~$800-1,500/yr (often lowest cost)
  • ChemTel — smaller player, legitimate

Critical: listing a commercial provider's number on your BoL without an active contract = federal violation. The provider won't accept the call if your account number isn't on file. See our CHEMTREC / 3E / INFOTRAC comparison for selecting the right provider.

Self-provided emergency response is legal per §172.604(a) but only practical for shippers with an internal 24/7 EHS on-call rotation that can meet the live-answer rule.

Display format

The emergency phone must be displayed either immediately after each hazmat shipping description or prominently displayed once on the BoL (larger font, highlighted, different color, or otherwise marked for quick and easy recognition). The free BoL Builder renders the phone in 14pt bold red.

§172.704 Hazmat Employee Training

Source: 49 CFR §172.704. Five training categories:

  1. General awareness / familiarization — recognizing hazmat and the HMR
  2. Function-specific — HMR requirements that apply to the employee's job (loading, signing BoL, classifying, securing)
  3. Safety — emergency response info, accident prevention
  4. Security awareness — recognizing transport security threats; within 90 days of hire
  5. In-depth security — required when hazmat is covered by a security plan under §172.802

Initial training timing: New hazmat employees may perform regulated functions before training is complete only under the direct supervision of a properly trained hazmat employee. Training must be completed within 90 days after employment or a change in job function.

Recurrent training: at least once every 3 years (in-depth security within 90 days of a security plan revision).

Recordkeeping per §172.704(d):

  • Employee name
  • Most recent training completion date
  • Description, copy, or location of training materials
  • Name and address of person providing the training
  • Certification that the employee has been trained and tested as required

Records retained as long as the employee is in hazmat employment AND for 90 days after termination. Available to DOT upon request. See our hazmat employee training deep-dive for who's a "hazmat employee" and who isn't.

Proper Shipping Description Format

Per 49 CFR §172.202, the description on the BoL must include, in order:

  1. Identification number (UN, NA, or ID)
  2. Proper shipping name
  3. Hazard class or division
  4. Packing group (where applicable)

Example: UN1203, Gasoline, 3, PG II. For tank car shipments, also include the technical name in parentheses when required by §172.203(k).

STCC Codes That Start with 49

Railinc maintains the Standard Transportation Commodity Code (STCC) catalog. Hazmat commodities use a separate STCC range starting with "49" (the Hazardous Materials STCC file). Non-hazmat STCC codes use other leading digits (01-48). The BoL must show the STCC because the railroad's waybill is rated against it. See our STCC vs NMFC vs HTS explainer for the relationships.

OT-55-R Key Train Trigger

Per AAR Circular OT-55-R (effective 2023-10-01), a train becomes a "Key Train" if it contains:

  • 20 or more loaded carloads of any combination of hazardous materials, OR
  • 1 or more carloads of poison-by-inhalation (PIH) or toxic-by-inhalation (TIH) material

Key Train operating restrictions: 50-mph maximum speed, roller-bearing cars only, route risk assessment required, additional protective measures during yard handling. This is the railroad's operating restriction, not a BoL field per se, but if your facility is loading multiple hazmat cars or any PIH/TIH commodity, the routing and lead time will reflect the Key Train rules.

2025 PHMSA Penalty Maximums

Effective January 2025, per Federal Register 2024-30608 (inflation-adjusted under 49 CFR 107.329 Appendix A):

Violation Type Minimum Maximum (per day)
General hazmat violation No minimum $99,756
Death, serious illness, severe injury, or substantial property destruction No minimum $238,809
Training-related violation $617 $99,756

Each day of a continuing violation is a separate offense. The penalty stack on a multi-car move can run into millions if the same defect appears on every car.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 49 CFR §172.204 require on a rail bill of lading?

§172.204 requires the shipper to sign a certification statement on the BoL for every hazmat shipment. The domestic certification is: "This is to certify that the above-named materials are properly classified, described, packaged, marked and labeled, and are in proper condition for transportation according to the applicable regulations of the Department of Transportation." An international alternative is also permitted. The signature may be manual, typewritten, mechanical, or for electronic transmission, the authorized person's name in a designated computer field.

What does §172.604 require for emergency response phone numbers?

§172.604 requires the shipper to provide a numeric emergency response telephone number, monitored 24/7 by a live person knowledgeable of the hazmat. Answering machines, answering services, beepers, and callback lines are explicitly prohibited. CHEMTREC (1-800-424-9300) is the industry standard provider, but the shipper must have an active contract; listing the number without subscription is a federal violation.

What are the 2025 PHMSA penalty maximums for hazmat violations on rail BoLs?

Effective January 2025: $99,756 per general hazmat violation per day, $238,809 per day when the violation results in death, serious illness, severe injury, or substantial property destruction, and a $617 minimum for training-related violations. Each day of continuing violation is a separate offense. Source: 49 CFR 107.329 Appendix A, inflation-adjusted per Federal Register 2024-30608.

Do I need CHEMTREC to ship hazmat by rail?

Not specifically CHEMTREC, but you do need a 24/7 live-answered emergency response phone meeting §172.604. CHEMTREC is the dominant commercial provider; alternatives include 3E (Verisk), INFOTRAC, and ChemTel. Self-provided is legal per §172.604(a) but only practical if you maintain an internal 24/7 EHS on-call rotation. Whatever number you list, you must have a contract with the provider or the live-answer infrastructure to back it.

What's the proper shipping name format on a rail BoL?

Per 49 CFR §172.202, the hazmat description must include, in order: identification number (UN, NA, or ID), proper shipping name, hazard class or division, and packing group where applicable. Example: "UN1203, Gasoline, 3, PG II." Tank car shipments also require the technical name in parentheses when triggered by §172.203(k).

What is a Key Train under AAR OT-55-R?

A Key Train is any train containing 20 or more loaded carloads of hazardous materials in combination, or 1 or more carloads of poison-by-inhalation (PIH) or toxic-by-inhalation (TIH) material. Operating restrictions per the 2023-10-01 OT-55-R revision include a 50-mph maximum speed, roller-bearing cars only, route risk assessment, and additional yard handling protections. This affects routing and lead time, not BoL fields directly.
