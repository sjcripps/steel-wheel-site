Rail Transload Services

Rail for the long haul, even when your dock has no track. Steel Wheel Logistics coordinates the facility, the rail leg, and the handoff.

What we do

Transloading moves your product between rail cars and trucks at a facility built for the transfer — grain to a truck at an elevator, plastic pellets from a hopper into a pneumatic trailer, steel from a gondola onto a flatbed. It’s how shippers get rail economics on lanes where one end has no siding. If the concept is new to you, start with our plain-English explainer: what is transloading?

The transfer itself happens at a facility. The work around the transfer is ours: Steel Wheel Logistics selects the facility, matches your commodity to its handling equipment, and coordinates the rail move and the handoff. We don’t operate terminals and we don’t take title to your freight — so the facility we point you to is the one that fits your move, not one we have to fill.

Who this is for

  • Your site isn’t rail-served. The plant, yard, or job site has no siding, but the lane is long enough that rail should be doing the heavy lifting.
  • Your customer is on a delivered basis in a market where you have no rail access — the transload is how the move becomes yours to control.
  • You’re weighing rail against truck and need the honest math: rail line-haul plus handling plus the short truck leg, against direct truck all the way. Sometimes truck wins. We’ll tell you when it does — run the numbers yourself with our rail-vs-truck calculator.

What we handle

  • Facility selection — from our directory of 2,500+ transload locations across the U.S., Canada, and Mexico, filtered by commodity and the handling equipment your product actually needs: bulk conveyors, liquid pumps, blower systems, scales, heated transfer.
  • Commodity-to-equipment matching — the right railcar on the rail side (our railcar selector is the self-serve version) and the right transfer method at the dock.
  • Rail leg coordination — carrier outreach, rate sourcing, and car supply for the line-haul into the transload point.
  • The handoff — coordinating timing between the railroad, the facility, and the final-mile carrier so cars don’t sit accruing charges. Estimate exposure with the demurrage calculator.
  • Lane math — an indicative rail rate estimate for the rail side, free, before you ever talk to us. It’s an estimate; we do not guarantee rates — real pricing comes from the carriers and the facility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are rail transload services?

Two jobs wearing one name. The facility physically moves product between railcars and trucks. The coordinator picks the right facility, arranges the rail leg, and manages the handoff. Steel Wheel provides the coordination — shipper’s side, no facility to fill, no title taken.

Which companies provide rail transload services?

Facility operators — railroad-affiliated networks and hundreds of independent terminals — run the physical sites. Coordinators like Steel Wheel Logistics work for the shipper: we select from 2,500+ facilities, match commodity to capability, and coordinate the move end to end. The distinction matters because a facility operator’s answer to “where should I transload?” is usually their own terminal.

When does transloading beat direct truck?

Generally: long line-haul, real volume, and a product that handles bulk transfer well. Short lanes and small volumes usually belong on trucks — the handling charge eats the rail saving. The honest comparison is rail line-haul + handling + short truck leg against truck all the way, and we’ll run it with you either way it comes out. Our transloading vs. direct rail guide covers the decision in depth.

How much does transloading cost?

Facilities quote their own handling rates — typically per ton or per unit, varying by commodity, equipment, and storage time. We don’t publish facility rates because they aren’t ours to publish; we help you get the quote and judge the total lane economics against it.

Can you find a transload facility near me?

That’s the everyday work. Start with the free directory — filter by your state and commodity — or tell us the lane and we’ll shortlist facilities that genuinely fit.

One-time move or ongoing program?

Both. One-time bulk moves can pencil when distance and volume are there — and when they don’t, we say so. Recurring lanes are where transloading earns its keep, and ongoing coordination is part of our outsourced rail department service.
