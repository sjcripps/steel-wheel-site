Salt Rail Shipping & Logistics: Rates, Railcars, Lanes

Rock salt from Midwest and Gulf dome mines — the road-deicing supply chain runs on rail and barge positioning done months before the first storm.

Salt logistics by rail: what we do

Steel Wheel Logistics prices and coordinates salt carload moves nationwide — single cars and multi-car blocks — from routing and an indicative rate per car through car ordering, release and tracing to placement. Salt weighs out at 100–112 tons per car (roughly 72–80 lb per cubic foot) well before the car is full, so we size covered or open-top hoppers to the weight limit and the receiving track's capacity rather than to cube. Typical end uses are road deicing (the big tonnage), water softening, chlor-alkali feedstock, food — we confirm the receiving track and unloading method before the first car is ordered. Where the plant has no spur, we set up transload at a bulk yard near the receiver and price the rail-plus-truck move against straight truck, so the mode decision is made on numbers. Demurrage control is planned rather than reactive: manifest cars arrive bunched, so we set release rules with the receiver and watch free time car by car. Shippers who need equipment can use our railcar brokerage to source covered or open-top hoppers on lease or per trip.

What it's used for

Road deicing (the big tonnage), water softening, chlor-alkali feedstock, food.

How it moves

Mines to municipal and DOT stockpiles across the snow belt, mostly staged in summer and fall. Deicing salt can ride open-tops; evaporated and food grades need covered cars.

Common questions

Can salt be shipped by rail?

Yes. Salt moves regularly on the North American rail network in covered or open-top hoppers. Mines to municipal and DOT stockpiles across the snow belt, mostly staged in summer and fall.

How many tons of salt fit in a railcar?

A typical load is 100–112 net tons per car — the car reaches its weight limit well before its cubic capacity.

What railcar does salt move in?

Covered or open-top hopper. Because the commodity is dense, smaller-cube cars are common — the weight limit governs.

What is the bulk density of salt?

Roughly 72–80 lb per cubic foot, depending on grade and condition.
