Iron Ore Rail Shipping & Logistics: Rates, Railcars, Lanes

Taconite pellets from Minnesota's Iron Range — so dense that purpose-built ore cars are barely half the length of a grain hopper and still hit the axle limit.

Iron ore logistics by rail: what we do

Steel Wheel Logistics prices and coordinates iron ore carload moves nationwide — single cars, multi-car blocks and unit-train sets — from routing and an indicative rate per car through car ordering, release and tracing to placement. Iron ore weighs out at 95–110 tons per car (roughly 130–150 lb per cubic foot) well before the car is full, so we size ore cars to the weight limit and the receiving track's capacity rather than to cube. Typical end uses are blast-furnace and DRI steelmaking — we confirm the receiving track and unloading method before the first car is ordered. Where the plant has no spur, we set up transload at a bulk yard near the receiver and price the rail-plus-truck move against straight truck, so the mode decision is made on numbers. Demurrage control is planned rather than reactive: on shuttle and unit-train cycles the clock runs on the whole set, so loading and release windows are agreed before the train is ordered. Shippers who need equipment can use our railcar brokerage to source ore cars on lease or per trip.

Common questions

Can iron ore be shipped by rail?

Yes. Iron Ore moves regularly on the North American rail network in ore carss. Captive unit trains from the Mesabi Range to Great Lakes ore docks, then lakers to lower-lakes steel mills; some all-rail moves to mills..

How many tons of iron ore fit in a railcar?

A typical load is 95–110 net tons per car — the car reaches its weight limit well before its cubic capacity.

What railcar does iron ore move in?

Ore cars (short) or open-top hopper. Because the commodity is dense, smaller-cube cars are common — the weight limit governs.

What is the bulk density of iron ore?

Roughly 130–150 lb per cubic foot, depending on grade and condition.
