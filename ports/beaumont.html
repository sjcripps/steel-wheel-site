Port of Beaumont, TX rail freight: what lands here and where it goes inland

The largest vessel import here, year to date through July 2026, is wood pulp (about $59 million) among the 22 commodity chapters Steel Wheel Logistics tracks. The port district is reached by CPKC, Union Pacific and BNSF Railway, which is what makes the Beaumont gateway useful for shippers whose plants sit hundreds of miles inland. This page pairs the Census import data behind the Commodity Flow Map port layer with the railroads, transload capacity and inland lanes that turn a vessel arrival into a rail move.

Typical inland lanes from this port

How the top import groups typically move inland from Beaumont. These describe the flow, not a price: run the indicative rail rate from Beaumont for a first number.

  • Imported market pulp moves in boxcars to tissue, paper and packaging mills in the Southeast, Midwest and Northeast. Indicative rail rate from Beaumont.
  • Bulk and break-bulk cargo unloaded at Beaumont terminals transfers to railcars at dockside or at a nearby transload facility and moves to inland consignees on CPKC, Union Pacific and BNSF Railway lanes. Indicative rail rate from Beaumont.
  • Railcars that deliver inland freight into Beaumont reposition with outbound export loads, so import lanes are often paired with export moves to keep equipment cycles balanced. Indicative rail rate from Beaumont.
  • Shippers consolidating imports from Beaumont with domestic production can route both through the same inland terminal, which simplifies car supply and switching. Indicative rail rate from Beaumont.
