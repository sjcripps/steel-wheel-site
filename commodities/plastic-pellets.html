Plastic Pellets Rail Shipping & Logistics: Rates, Railcars, Lanes

Polyethylene and polypropylene pellets from Gulf Coast crackers — light, free-flowing, and shipped in the biggest covered hoppers on the railroad.

Plastic pellets logistics by rail: what we do

Steel Wheel Logistics prices and coordinates plastic pellets carload moves nationwide — single cars and multi-car blocks — from routing and an indicative rate per car through car ordering, release and tracing to placement. Plastic pellets fill the car before they reach the weight limit (about 32–38 lb per cubic foot), topping out near 85–95 tons, so we spec the largest-cube jumbo covered hoppers the lane allows and check the load plan against the receiver's unloading setup. Typical end uses are injection molding, film, pipe, packaging — the feedstock of the plastics industry — we confirm the receiving track and unloading method before the first car is ordered. Where the plant has no spur, we set up transload at a bulk transload terminal near the receiver and price the rail-plus-truck move against straight truck, so the mode decision is made on numbers. Demurrage control is planned rather than reactive: manifest cars arrive bunched, so we set release rules with the receiver and watch free time car by car. Shippers who need equipment can use our railcar brokerage to source jumbo covered hoppers on lease or per trip.

Common questions

Can plastic pellets (resin) be shipped by rail?

Yes. Plastic Pellets (Resin) moves regularly on the North American rail network in jumbo covered hoppers. Gulf Coast petrochemical belt to converters nationwide; cars stage in massive storage-in-transit yards near Houston.

How many tons of plastic pellets (resin) fit in a railcar?

A typical load is 85–95 net tons per car — the car fills up (cubes out) before it reaches its weight limit.

What railcar does plastic pellets (resin) move in?

Jumbo covered hopper (6,000+ cu ft). Because the commodity is light for its volume, shippers prefer the largest cubic capacity available.

What is the bulk density of plastic pellets (resin)?

Roughly 32–38 lb per cubic foot, depending on grade and condition.
