Pricing Fundamentals

STCC codes, rate types, and the components of your rail invoice

STCC Codes: The DNA of Rail Pricing

  • Standard Transportation Commodity Code — 7-digit number assigned to every product
  • First 2 digits = broad commodity group (01=Farm, 28=Chemicals, 32=Stone/Glass)
  • Remaining digits narrow to specific product (2818121 = Sodium Hydroxide, liquid)
  • Your STCC code determines: applicable tariff rates, equipment requirements, routing
  • WRONG STCC = wrong rate, wrong car, potential regulatory violation
  • Hazmat commodities have special STCC prefixes (49 = Hazardous Materials)
  • Railroads use STCC to benchmark revenue and set competitive pricing
  • Always verify your STCC with the railroad BEFORE shipping — disputes are expensive

Contract Rates vs

  • Tariff (Common Carrier) Rates
  • Contract Rates
  • Negotiated directly with railroad
  • Typically 1-3 year terms
  • Volume commitment required. 20-50% below tariff rates
  • Confidential (not public)
  • Performance guarantees possible
  • Best for: steady, predictable volume
  • Tariff (Published) Rates
  • Published common carrier rates
  • Available to anyone — no commitment
  • Highest price per car
  • No volume minimum
  • Filed with STB (legally required)
  • Serve as ceiling / benchmark
  • Best for: occasional or new shippers

Anatomy of a Rail Freight Invoice

  • LINE HAUL CHARGE — The base transportation rate (origin to destination)
  • FUEL SURCHARGE — Variable charge indexed to diesel price (adds 10-25%)
  • SWITCHING CHARGE — Fee for switching cars at origin/destination ($200-$500/car)
  • DEMURRAGE — Penalty for holding cars beyond free time ($75-$300/day/car)
  • WEIGHING CHARGE — Scale fee if weight verification required ($25-$75/car)
  • DIVERSION CHARGE — Fee to change destination while car is in transit ($100-$400)
  • RECONSIGNMENT — Fee to change consignee after shipment ($75-$250)
  • The line haul is often only 60-70% of your total invoice — watch the accessorials!

Fuel Surcharges: How They Work

  • The Mechanism
  • Indexed to US DOE weekly diesel price
  • Each railroad has its own FSC table
  • Typically: base rate x FSC percentage
  • Calculated per car or per ton
  • Updated weekly or monthly
  • Adds 10-25% to your base rate
  • Some contracts cap FSC exposure
  • What You Can Negotiate
  • FSC cap (maximum % regardless of diesel)
  • FSC floor (minimum diesel before FSC applies)
  • Alternative index (WTI crude vs DOE diesel)
  • Fuel efficiency credit (rail vs truck savings)
  • Fixed FSC in exchange for longer commitment
  • CRITICAL: FSC is often where margin hides
  • Always model FSC at $4, $5, and $6/gal diesel
Part B

How Railroads Set Prices

Understanding their economics to negotiate better rates

R/VC Ratio: How Railroads Measure Pricing Power

  • R/VC Ratio = Revenue / Variable Cost of providing the service
  • R/VC of 180% means the railroad earns 1.8x its variable cost on that shipment
  • STB considers R/VC > 180% as potentially 'revenue adequate' (railroad making enough)
  • Average R/VC across the industry: ~200-250% (railroads earn well above variable cost)
  • Captive shippers (only one railroad option) see R/VC of 300%+ — limited negotiating power
  • Competitive lanes (2+ railroad options) see R/VC closer to 150-180%
  • KNOW YOUR R/VC — it tells you how much pricing power the railroad has over YOUR freight
  • STB Quarterly Commodity Statistics (QCS) publishes actual R/VC data by commodity and route

Captive Shippers vs

  • Competitive Shippers
  • Captive Shippers
  • Only one railroad serves your facility
  • No alternative rail carrier available
  • Railroad has maximum pricing power
  • R/VC ratios: 250-400%+
  • STB rate challenge is your main recourse
  • Building a second railroad connection costs $2-10M+
  • Truck alternative sets a ceiling (but a high one)
  • Competitive Shippers
  • Two or more railroads serve your facility
  • Or: realistic transload option to second carrier
  • Railroad must compete on price AND service
  • R/VC ratios: 150-200%
  • Negotiate aggressively — they need your freight
  • Play carriers against each other (carefully)
  • Best position in all of rail freight

Volume Pricing: Unit Train vs

  • Single Carload. $3,500-$5,000
  • One car at a time
  • Manifest train routing
  • Multiple yard stops
  • Highest cost per ton
  • Most switching charges
  • Multi-Car Block. $2,800-$4,000. 5-25 cars together
  • Block discount: 10-20%
  • Stays together through yards
  • Fewer handling touches
  • Popular for chemicals
  • Unit Train. $1,500-$2,500. 80-135 cars, same commodity
  • No yard stops = lower cost. 30-50% below manifest
  • Requires shipper infrastructure
  • Grain, coal, crude, potash
  • Shuttle Train. $1,200-$2,000
  • Dedicated consist cycling
  • Lowest possible rate. 40-60% below manifest
  • Requires 100+ car loop track
  • Grain + ethanol primarily

Contract Negotiation Tactics That Work

  • KNOW YOUR DATA — Download STB QCS data for your commodity/route before negotiating
  • BENCHMARK — Compare your rate to published averages (R/VC ratio, cost per ton-mile)
  • VOLUME COMMITMENT — Offer guaranteed minimums for rate concessions (and HONOR them)
  • MULTI-YEAR TERM — 3-year deals with annual escalators beat renegotiating annually
  • PERFORMANCE GUARANTEES — Tie rate concessions to transit time commitments
  • FUEL SURCHARGE CAP — Negotiate a ceiling on FSC exposure
  • COMPETITIVE ALTERNATIVES — Have a real Plan B (second carrier, truck, transload)
  • BUILD THE RELATIONSHIP — Railroad reps have discretion
  • Don't burn bridges

STB Data: Your Secret Weapon

  • Surface Transportation Board publishes the Carload Waybill Sample (CWS)
  • Quarterly Freight Commodity Statistics (QCS) — free, public data
  • Contains: revenue per ton, revenue per car, R/VC ratios by commodity
  • Broken down by: STCC code, origin/destination state, railroad, car type
  • Use it to: benchmark your rates, identify if you're paying above market
  • Also available: Confidential CWS data (requires application, more granular)
  • Tools: STB website, USDA Agricultural Transportation open data
  • No railroad wants you to know this data exists — now you do

Common Pricing Mistakes (Avoid These)

Accepting the first rate quote without benchmarking. Ignoring fuel surcharges in your total cost analysis. Not tracking accessorial charges (they add up to 30-40% of base rate). Signing a contract without volume flexibility (take-or-pay traps). Failing to document service failures (you need data to renegotiate). Negotiating rate only, not service level (cheap rate + bad service = worse). Not understanding your R/VC position (captive vs competitive). Letting contracts auto-renew without renegotiating.

Pricing Checkpoint

✓ You can explain what an STCC code is and why it matters. ✓ You know the difference between contract and tariff rates. ✓ You can identify every line item on a rail freight invoice. ✓ You understand fuel surcharges and how to negotiate caps. ✓ You know your R/VC ratio position (captive vs. competitive). ✓ You can use STB data to benchmark your rates. ✓ You have negotiation tactics ready for your next contract discussion.

Key Terms (1 of 2)

  • STCC Code. 7-digit Standard Transportation Commodity Code classifying products
  • Contract Rate
  • Confidential, negotiated rate with volume commitment
  • Tariff Rate
  • Published common carrier rate; available to all, highest price
  • Fuel Surcharge (FSC)
  • Variable charge indexed to diesel price; adds 10-25% to base
  • The base transportation charge from origin to destination
  • Revenue-to-Variable-Cost ratio; measures railroad pricing power
  • Captive Shipper
  • Shipper served by only one railroad; limited negotiating leverage
  • Accessorial Charges
  • All charges beyond line haul: switching, demurrage, weighing, etc

Key Terms (2 of 2)

  • Surface Transportation Board — federal agency regulating railroad rates
  • Quarterly Commodity Statistics — STB-published rate benchmarking data
  • Shipping document with origin, destination, commodity, weight, rate
  • Changing a car's destination while in transit (incurs a fee)
  • Reconsignment
  • Changing the consignee/receiver after shipment begins
  • Take-or-Pay
  • Contract clause requiring payment even if volume isn't shipped
  • Multi-Car Block
  • Group of 5-25 cars shipping together for volume discount
  • Common Carrier Obligation
  • Railroad's legal duty to provide service at published rates

Practical Assignment

  • Step 1: Pull your last 12 months of rail freight invoices
  • Step 2: Categorize all charges: line haul, FSC, switching, demurrage, other accessorials
  • Step 3: Calculate the TRUE total cost per car and per ton-mile for each lane
  • Step 4: Download STB QCS data for your commodity group (stb.gov/stb/industry/econ-waybill.html)
  • Step 5: Benchmark your rates against the STB averages — are you above or below?
  • Step 6: Identify your top 3 lanes by spend and calculate the R/VC ratio for each
  • Step 7: Prepare a negotiation brief: current rate, benchmark, proposed rate, volume offer
  • Step 8: Schedule a meeting with your railroad's pricing/sales representative

Key Takeaways

✅ STCC codes drive everything — verify yours is correct before it costs you. ✅ Contract rates save 20-50% over tariff — if you have the volume to commit. ✅ Fuel surcharges are negotiable — always cap your exposure. ✅ Accessorial charges can add 30-40% to your base rate — track every penny. ✅ Volume is your best leverage: unit trains save 30-50% over manifest carloads. ✅ Know your R/VC ratio — it tells you exactly how much negotiating room you have. ✅ STB data is free and public — use it to benchmark every rate you're paying. ✅ The best negotiation combines data, volume commitment, and a credible alternative.
