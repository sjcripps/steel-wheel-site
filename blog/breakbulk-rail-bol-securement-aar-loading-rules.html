Breakbulk Rail BoL + AAR Loading Rules: Securement Compliance (2026)

May 16, 2026 · 8 min read · Operations
Key fact: Breakbulk and open-top rail shipments require the BoL to reference the applicable AAR loading rule for securement compliance. "Loaded and braced in accordance with AAR [Open Top Loading Rules Section X / Closed Car Pamphlet Y / Pamphlet 34 for tank cars]" on the special instructions block is the standard language. The free BoL Builder auto-populates this when breakbulk mode is enabled.

When you ship steel coil on a gondola, lumber on a centerbeam flatcar, or pipe on a bulkhead flatcar, the cargo securement is governed by the AAR Open Top Loading Rules. When you ship machinery on a boxcar floor, AAR Closed Car Rules apply. Tank cars get Pamphlet 34. The BoL has to reference the specific rule the load complied with. Here's the mapping and the language.

The AAR Loading Rules Library

The Association of American Railroads (AAR) publishes loading and securement standards for each equipment type and cargo profile. The main references:

Equipment / Cargo AAR Reference
Open-top railcar (gondola, flatcar, mill gondola, well car, bulkhead flatcar)AAR Open Top Loading Rules Manual (OTLR), specific section by cargo
Closed boxcar / refrigerator carAAR Closed Car Loading Rules (Pamphlet series)
Non-pressure and pressure tank carsAAR Pamphlet 34 (Recommended Methods for the Safe Loading and Unloading of Non-Pressure & Pressure Tank Cars)
Hazmat Key Train operationsAAR Circular OT-55-R (eff. 2023-10-01)
US rail hazmat instructions (consolidated)AAR US Hazmat Instructions for Rail (Jan 2022 update)

What to Render on the BoL

On the special instructions block of the BoL, render the verbatim securement reference. Common patterns:

Steel coil on gondola:

"Loaded and braced in accordance with AAR Open Top Loading Rules Section 1 (Coil Steel)."

Lumber on centerbeam flatcar:

"Loaded and braced in accordance with AAR Open Top Loading Rules Section 6 (Lumber and Forest Products)."

Pipe on bulkhead flatcar:

"Loaded and braced in accordance with AAR Open Top Loading Rules Section 3 (Pipe)."

Hazmat tank car:

"Loaded in accordance with AAR Pamphlet 34 (Safe Loading and Unloading of Non-Pressure and Pressure Tank Cars)."

OT-55-R Key Train Considerations

If your hazmat lines tip the train into Key Train status (20+ loaded hazmat carloads OR 1+ PIH/TIH carload), AAR Circular OT-55-R operating restrictions kick in:

  • 50-mph maximum speed
  • Roller-bearing cars only
  • Route risk assessment required
  • Additional yard handling protections

OT-55-R is an operating restriction on the railroad, not a BoL field per se. But the BoL drives whether the carrier classifies the train as a Key Train. Surface accurate hazmat counts and STCC codes so the railroad's routing engine identifies the trigger correctly.

Why This Matters

Three concrete reasons securement compliance language goes on the BoL:

  1. Carrier acceptance. The originating railroad may refuse to accept a breakbulk car without a securement reference on the BoL. The reference shows the shipper followed an established rule rather than improvising.
  2. Inspection authority. Railroad mechanical inspectors check loads against the cited rule. A securement that doesn't match the referenced rule fails inspection.
  3. Carmack defense. If cargo shifts in transit and damage results, the shipper's compliance with the AAR rule is a defense element. Without the reference, the shipper has a harder time showing it loaded to industry standard.

Finding the Right Section Number

The AAR Open Top Loading Rules Manual is organized by cargo type. Each section covers a specific commodity profile (coils, lumber, pipe, plate, structural shapes, machinery, military equipment, etc.). The full manual is gated to AAR/Railinc members; non-member shippers usually work from their carrier's published references or from secondary references provided by industry trade groups.

Closed Car Loading

For boxcar / refrigerator car shipments, AAR Closed Car Loading Rules (Pamphlets) cover dunnage, blocking, and bracing for various cargo types. The BoL language follows the same pattern: "Loaded in accordance with AAR Closed Car Loading Rules Pamphlet [number]."

Tank Car Loading

AAR Pamphlet 34 is the consolidated reference for non-pressure and pressure tank cars. Covers loading procedures, securement of valves and fittings, unloading safety, and emergency response. Hazmat tank cars cite Pamphlet 34 in the special instructions block along with the §172.604 emergency phone, §172.204 certification, and §172.704 training acknowledgment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What AAR loading rule do I reference on a breakbulk rail BoL?

Depends on the equipment and cargo. Open-top cars (gondolas, flatcars) reference the AAR Open Top Loading Rules Manual section for the specific cargo (coils, lumber, pipe, etc.). Closed boxcars reference AAR Closed Car Loading Rules Pamphlets. Non-pressure and pressure tank cars reference AAR Pamphlet 34. The BoL language is: "Loaded and braced in accordance with AAR [reference]."

What does AAR Pamphlet 34 cover?

AAR Pamphlet 34 is the consolidated reference for the safe loading and unloading of non-pressure and pressure tank cars. Covers loading procedures, securement of valves and fittings, unloading safety, and emergency response. Required reference on hazmat tank car BoLs in the special instructions block.

What is OT-55-R and when does it apply?

AAR Circular OT-55-R (effective 2023-10-01) is the Key Train operating rule. A train becomes a Key Train when it contains 20 or more loaded hazmat carloads in any combination OR 1 or more carloads of poison-by-inhalation (PIH) / toxic-by-inhalation (TIH) material. Operating restrictions: 50-mph max speed, roller-bearing cars only, route risk assessment, additional yard handling protections. Surface accurate hazmat counts on the BoL so the carrier's routing engine identifies the trigger.

Why does the BoL need to reference the AAR loading rule?

Three reasons: (1) the originating railroad may refuse to accept a breakbulk car without a securement reference; (2) railroad mechanical inspectors check loads against the cited rule; (3) it's a Carmack defense element if cargo shifts in transit and damage results — compliance with an AAR rule shows the shipper followed industry standard.

Where can I get the AAR Open Top Loading Rules manual?

The full manual is gated to AAR / Railinc members. Non-member shippers typically work from their carrier's published references, from secondary references provided by industry trade groups (NRC, ASLRRA), or from the AAR's public standards page at aar.com/standards/OpenTop.php. Membership is required for the full canonical document.
