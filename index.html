What We Do

What a Rail Logistics Company Does for You

Everything an in-house rail department would do, without hiring one. Engage a piece at a time or hand off the whole function.

Routing & Pricing

We route the lane, source the real railroad rate, and tell you what the move should cost before you commit. Start with an indicative rail freight quote, then we firm it up with the carrier. See current rail freight rates by lane for what a carload costs this month.

Railroad Coordination

We deal with the Class I railroads and short lines on your behalf: carrier onboarding, car ordering, service issues, and the day-to-day calls nobody on your team has time for.

Transload & Equipment

Rail-to-truck handoffs when a site isn’t rail-served, plus car tracking, pool ordering, and diversions whether you run private cars or railroad equipment.

Demurrage Control

The number one surprise cost on rail. We pre-plan car supply and loading windows, find the root cause of charges, and open the right conversation with the railroad on the ones that shouldn’t stand.

Railcar Brokerage

As a railcar broker we source lease and sublease cars, place your surplus cars, review lease renewals against the market, and pick the right car type before you shop.

The Whole Rail Function

All of the above, ongoing, as your outsourced rail department. One call a week instead of six people learning rail on the side.

About Us

A Rail Logistics Company, Not a Generalist

Steel Wheel Logistics does one thing: rail. We don’t cover every mode and we don’t haul the freight. We coordinate bulk commodity moves with the Class I carriers and short lines, and on the equipment side we work as a railcar broker. We never take title to your freight, so your dollars stay in your supply chain.

The company is run by Jacob Cripps, with about 25 years in rail operations, out of Ridgeland, Mississippi. When you call (601) 821-2199, that is who answers.

No rail person on staff? We become one. Steel Wheel runs as your outsourced rail department — the whole rail function, handled.

Cities We Serve

Rail Freight Coverage in Major U.S. Hubs

City-by-city guides to local railroads, terminals, port access, and dominant commodity flows. View all 50 cities →

Chicago, IL

The largest rail hub in North America — six Class I carriers interchange here daily, with Joliet/Logistics Park as the dominant terminal complex.

Houston, TX

Gulf Coast petrochemical capital — BNSF, UP, and KCS coverage with deep-water port access for plastic resin, NGLs, and chemical exports.

Los Angeles, CA

San Pedro Bay rail gateway — BNSF and UP service, ICTF and on-dock rail at the Ports of LA/Long Beach for Pacific imports.

Dallas, TX

BNSF Alliance and UP’s Dallas terminal — transcontinental crossroads for plastics, steel, and consumer goods moving north-south.

Atlanta, GA

CSX Hulsey and NS Inman Yard — the Southeast's primary rail interchange, dominant in automotive, paper, and building materials.

Memphis, TN

Five Class I carriers at America's distribution center — BNSF, UP, NS, CSX, and CN converge with Mississippi River barge interchange.

New Orleans, LA

Six Class I rail-to-water gateway — bulk grain, petrochemical, and steel exports through the Lower Mississippi port complex.

Kansas City, MO

Second-largest rail hub by tonnage — KCS, BNSF, UP, NS, and CPKC interchange for grain, ag products, and Mexico-bound traffic.

St. Louis, MO

Mississippi River rail gateway — six Class I carriers serve the Metro East terminals for coal, grain, steel, and aggregate flows.

Minneapolis, MN

Twin Cities rail hub — BNSF Northtown Yard and CP Shoreham serve grain country with CN and UP interchange to the south.

Nashville, TN

CSX Radnor and the South's auto corridor — major flows for steel, automotive parts, and building materials with Class I interchange.

Charlotte, NC

NS Charlotte terminal — Carolinas distribution hub with CSX interchange and direct lanes to Atlanta and the Northeast.
