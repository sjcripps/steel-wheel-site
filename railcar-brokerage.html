Steel Wheel Logistics sources, places, and renews railcars for shippers who need equipment without building an equipment desk.

What a railcar broker does

A railcar broker sits between the shippers who need cars and the lessors, lessees, and owners who have them. The railcar market is thin and mostly private: the good cars move by phone call, not by listing, and the rate you get depends on who you know and how well you can describe what you need. Steel Wheel Logistics works that market for you. We do not own a fleet, so we have no reason to steer you toward one lessor’s cars over another’s.

  • Sourcing lease and sublease cars. Full-service and net leases from the major lessors, plus sublease cars from lessees who have surplus. We check both, because the sublease market often has the right car type available sooner and cheaper.
  • Matching surplus cars to demand. If you are paying lease on cars you are not loading, we place them with a shipper who will, so the idle rent stops.
  • Lease renewal review. Before you sign a renewal, we benchmark the offered rate against current market activity and the car’s remaining useful life, and tell you plainly whether to sign, negotiate, or return the cars.
  • Car type selection. Covered hopper, tank, gondola, boxcar, flatcar, and the sub-types inside each. The wrong car costs you on capacity, on loading, and on the lease rate. We settle the spec before you shop.

Who uses a railcar broker

  • Shippers with a shortage. Volume is up, the railroad pool cannot keep up, and you need cars in the next 30 to 90 days rather than the next lease cycle.
  • Shippers with a surplus. Volume is down or a contract ended, and you are paying rent on cars that sit. A sublease placement turns that cost into an offset.
  • Lessees near renewal. The lessor’s renewal letter arrived and you have no independent read on whether the rate is fair. A renewal review gives you one before you sign.
  • New-to-rail shippers needing first cars. You have a lane that belongs on rail and no equipment. We pick the car type, size the fleet, and source the first cars alongside the rest of your rail setup.

How engagement works

  • 1. Call. A 30-minute call to hear what you move, where it goes, and what equipment you have or need. Book it here or call (601) 821-2199.
  • 2. Requirements. We write down the spec: car type, capacity, count, term, delivery window, loading and unloading constraints, and whether the cars need to be clean, lined, or certified for your commodity.
  • 3. Market check. We work the lessors, sublease sources, and our own network against that spec and against the current lease-rate market.
  • 4. Options. You get a short list with rate, term, condition, and availability side by side, and a plain recommendation. No option is hidden to make another look better.
  • 5. Execution. We help you through the lease or sublease paperwork, the inspection, and the first delivery, and stay on the equipment side as long as you want us there.

Scope is quoted before work starts. Market figures on this site are indicative; every rate you sign comes from the lessor or sublessor, not from us.
