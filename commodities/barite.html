Barite by Rail

Barium sulfate — the weighting agent in oil-well drilling mud. API drilling grade requires specific gravity of 4.2+, so a car hits its weight limit at roughly a quarter of its cube.

RailcarOpen-top or covered hopper
Typical net load100–110 tons per car
Bulk density~130–160 (crude lump) lb/cu ft
Loading constraintWeighs out (weight-limited)
STCC14711 — look up related codes in the STCC Finder

What it's used for

Drilling-mud weighting (the overwhelming majority), plus fillers and radiation shielding.

How it moves

Crude ore rails from mines and import terminals to grinding plants near the oil patch (west Texas, Gulf Coast, Oklahoma); ground API barite then trucks or rails to mud plants. Ground product must stay dry — covered equipment.

Common questions

Can barite be shipped by rail?

Yes. Barite moves regularly on the North American rail network in open-top or covered hoppers. Crude ore rails from mines and import terminals to grinding plants near the oil patch (west Texas, Gulf Coast, Oklahoma); ground API barite then trucks or rails to mud plants.

How many tons of barite fit in a railcar?

A typical load is 100–110 net tons per car — the car reaches its weight limit well before its cubic capacity.

What railcar does barite move in?

Open-top or covered hopper. Because the commodity is dense, smaller-cube cars are common — the weight limit governs.

What is the bulk density of barite?

Roughly 130–160 (crude lump) lb per cubic foot, depending on grade and condition.
