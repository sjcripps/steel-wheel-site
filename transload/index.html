Transload facilities and transloading services in the U.S.

A transload facility is a rail-served site where freight is moved between railcars and trucks. It has the track, the transfer equipment and the truck access, so a shipper or receiver can use rail for the long haul without a rail spur of their own. This page lists 2,785 rail transload locations across 70 U.S. states, Canadian provinces and Mexican states, browsable by state and city.

Transloading services are what happens at that site: rail-to-truck and truck-to-rail transfer, dry bulk handling (conveyors, pneumatic transfer, dump pits), liquid bulk handling (pumps, tank storage, heated transfer), break-bulk handling of steel, lumber and paper, short or long-term storage, and truck loadout with scale tickets. Not every facility does all of it; the directory records the commodities and capabilities each operator publishes.

How a shipper uses one: the railcar is billed to the transload facility instead of to your dock. The operator unloads it into storage or straight into trucks, and the product covers the last 25–75 miles by road. Outbound works in reverse: trucks bring product to the facility, it is loaded into railcars there, and rail carries the long haul. No siding, no track agreement, no railcar spotting at your site.

How transloading works

  1. Pick the facility. Close enough to the origin or destination that the truck leg stays short, on a railroad that reaches your other end, with the equipment your commodity needs (pneumatic for pellets and cement, conveyor or dump pit for aggregates, pumps for liquids, cranes or forklifts for steel and lumber).
  2. Bill the rail leg to the facility. The railcar moves from origin to the transload site’s track under the facility’s name, with your product on the waybill. For an outbound move the facility is the origin instead.
  3. Transfer. The operator unloads the railcar into storage (silo, tank, warehouse, pad) or straight into waiting trucks. Straight-to-truck is cheapest but needs trucks lined up the day the car arrives.
  4. Store if needed. Most facilities include a few free days, then charge storage. Holding product in the railcar instead runs up railroad demurrage, which is the expensive way to store anything; the demurrage calculator shows the exposure.
  5. Truck loadout and delivery. Trucks are loaded, weighed and ticketed at the facility, then run the last miles to the consignee. Either you arrange the trucks or the facility does; agree on that before the first car ships.

What transloading costs

A transload move has three cost parts: the rail line-haul to the transload point, the facility’s handling fee, and the truck leg to the final destination. Our Transloading course module teaches the math with these working ranges: a handling fee of roughly $15–$35 per ton depending on commodity, a truck leg at $3–$5 per loaded mile over a typical 25–75 miles (priced per truck, not per ton), and all-truck at roughly $0.12–$0.20 per ton-mile for comparison. Those are teaching figures and indicative only; every facility quotes its own rate.

What moves the handling fee: the commodity (free-flowing grain is cheaper to handle than sticky or hazardous product), the equipment it needs (a pneumatic blower or a heated tank costs more per hour than a front-end loader), storage days beyond the free period, and the truck leg (distance, and whether the facility can load trucks fast enough to keep them moving). Run the rail side through the Rail Rate Quote tool, compare the whole lane with the rail-vs-truck calculator, and read transloading vs. direct rail for when the answer is “just truck it.”

Transload truck loadout

Truck loadout is the last step at the facility: product goes from the railcar or from storage into the truck, gets weighed, and leaves with a scale ticket. The equipment sets the pace. Pneumatic loadout for pellets, cement and fly ash blows product into a dry-bulk trailer; aggregates and grain load by conveyor, loadout spout or front-end loader into dump or hopper trailers; liquids pump into tank trailers; steel and lumber lift by crane or forklift onto flatbeds. A facility that loads out 40 trucks a day empties a 100-ton railcar in a morning; one that loads out eight leaves the car sitting, and that shows up as demurrage or storage.

Before committing a lane, ask the operator four things: loadout hours and whether they take appointments, trucks per hour for your commodity, whether there is a certified scale on site, and whether they arrange the trucks or you do. The answers decide the real cost of the truck leg and how many railcars you can push through the facility in a week. We ask those questions for every facility we shortlist.

Transload facility FAQ

What is a transload facility?

A transload facility is a rail-served site with track, transfer equipment and truck access where freight is moved between railcars and trucks. It lets a shipper or receiver use rail for the long haul without a rail spur of their own. Most handle dry bulk, liquid bulk or break-bulk, and many also offer storage and truck loadout.

How do I find a transload facility near me?

Start with the state grid on this page, or filter the directory tool by state, commodity and on-site capability. Look within roughly 25 to 75 miles of the origin or destination; beyond that the truck leg starts eating the rail saving. Or tell us the lane and the commodity and we shortlist the facilities that actually handle it.

Do I need a rail spur to use a transload facility?

No. That is the point of transloading: the facility has the track, so your product rides rail to the transload point and covers the last stretch by truck. A receiver with no siding can still buy on rail economics, and a shipper with no siding can still sell into rail-served markets.

What commodities can be transloaded?

Dry bulk (grain, feed, fertilizer, cement, fly ash, aggregates, frac sand, plastic pellets), liquid bulk (chemicals, petroleum products, vegetable oils, ethanol), break-bulk (steel, lumber, paper and wood products) and heavy or project cargo. What a given facility can take depends on its equipment: pneumatic transfer, conveyors, liquid pumps, scales, heated transfer, cranes or a flat pad for boxed cargo.

How do I price a transload move?

Add three parts: the rail line-haul to the transload point, the facility’s handling fee (usually quoted per ton or per unit) and the truck leg to the final destination, then compare the sum against direct truck for the same lane. The Rail Rate Quote tool gives an indicative rail figure, the facility quotes its own handling rate, and we put the three together and confirm them with the carrier and the operator.
